# FAANG Interview Guide

## Introduction

Getting an interview at a FAANG (Facebook/Meta, Amazon, Apple, Netflix, Google) or Tier-1 tech company is difficult, but passing the interview is entirely learnable. 

These companies have standardized their interview processes over the last decade. They test for specific signals, follow a strict rubric, and expect candidates to behave in a very specific way.

If you understand the "game" of the FAANG interview, you can drastically increase your offer rate.

---

## The Standard Loop

While every company differs slightly, a typical FAANG loop consists of:
1. **Online Assessment (OA):** An automated coding test (HackerRank, CodeSignal).
2. **Phone Screen:** A 45-minute coding interview with an engineer over a shared code editor (CoderPad).
3. **Onsite Loop:** A grueling 4 to 5 hour gauntlet consisting of:
   - 2 or 3 Coding Rounds
   - 1 System Design Round (for Mid-level/Senior roles)
   - 1 Behavioral / Cultural Fit Round

---

## What are they actually testing?

Contrary to popular belief, FAANG companies are not looking for solitary geniuses who can write $O(1)$ space algorithms in 5 minutes without speaking.

They are evaluating **Hire Signals**:
- Can this person write clean, readable, bug-free code?
- Can this person communicate complex technical trade-offs?
- How does this person react when they are stuck? Do they panic, or do they collaborate?
- Is this person a toxic "brilliant jerk", or a supportive team player?

---

## Key Takeaways

- The FAANG interview is a standardized, highly learnable process.
- Communication is just as important as the code you write.
- The onsite loop evaluates you as a coworker, not just a compiler.
